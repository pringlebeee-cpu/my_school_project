What is computational thinking?

Computational thinking is a problem-solving process. Even though the word “computational” sounds like it is only about computers, these ideas can be used without a computer too.

THE BASICS

The 4 parts

These four ideas work together to help turn a big problem into something easier to solve.

01▦

Decomposition

Breaking a large or complicated problem into smaller parts so each part is easier to understand and solve.

Example: Build a website by making the homepage, navigation, and sections separately.
At school: Break a project into research, writing, designing, and presenting.
02⌁

Pattern Recognition

Looking for things that repeat or are similar. Patterns can help you understand a problem and solve similar problems faster.

Example: Notice that several math problems use the same steps.
At school: Find repeated ideas in a science experiment or history timeline.
03◉

Abstraction

Focusing on the most important information while leaving out details that are not needed.

Example: A map shows important roads and locations without showing every detail of a city.
At school: Focus on main ideas instead of trying to memorize every detail.
04▶

Algorithms

Creating a clear set of steps or instructions that can be followed to solve a problem or complete a task.

Example: Following the steps in a recipe to make food.
At school: Make a step-by-step plan for a project or problem.

REAL LIFE

Computational thinking is everywhere.

You can use these skills even when you are not sitting at a computer.

💻

Coding

Programmers break projects into smaller tasks, find patterns, and create algorithms.

🍳

Cooking

A recipe is an algorithm. You follow steps in an order to reach a final result.

🎮

Gaming

Players recognize patterns, plan moves, and break difficult challenges into smaller goals.

📚

School

Students can use computational thinking when solving math problems, researching, or planning projects.

🗺️

Planning

A trip can be broken into transportation, places, timing, and other smaller tasks.

🧩

Everyday problems

Breaking a difficult situation into smaller steps can make it easier to handle.

WHY IT MATTERS

Why learn it?

01

Better problem solving

It gives you a process for handling complicated problems instead of trying to solve everything at once.

02

Useful in many subjects

It can be used in computer science, math, science, writing, and everyday situations.

03

Builds useful skills

It can help you practice logical thinking, organization, creativity, and planning.

!

There can be challenges too.

Learning a new way to solve problems can take practice. Some problems also do not have one simple solution, so you may need to try different approaches.
